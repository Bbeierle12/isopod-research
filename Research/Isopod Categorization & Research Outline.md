# Isopod Categorization & Research — Master Outline

A survey of the **many ways to categorize isopods** and the **methods used to research
them**, intended as the intellectual scaffold for organizing this vault. Each scheme is an
orthogonal *axis*: different research questions demand different categorizations, and the
strongest vaults let you slice the same species set along several axes at once.

> [!note] Scope
> "Isopod" here means the whole order **Isopoda** (~10,000+ species), not only terrestrial
> woodlice (suborder **Oniscidea**). Many schemes below are most developed for Oniscidea but
> generalize.

> [!check] Verification status (2026-07-25)
> This outline was cross-checked against two external research documents (in `Research/sources/`):
> a 13-axis framework paper and a citation-verification document. Corrections applied inline are
> marked with `> [!warning] … (corrected 2026-07-25)`. Bottom line: the framework is sound; the
> main fixes were (1) the Diplocheta→Crinocheta grade sequence is *not* settled phylogeny
> (Oniscidea monophyly contested), (2) *P. scaber* is a de-facto ecotox model with **no** ratified
> ISO/OECD guideline, and (3) terrestrial isopods are not true xylophages. Per-species ecology
> data (habitat stratum, trophic guild, life-history) with **evidence grades** lives in
> [[Isopod Species Ecology Data]].
>
> **Evidence grading convention** (carry into the vault; do *not* let inference harden into fact):
> **a** = published fact · **b** = inference from close relatives · **c** = genuinely unstudied.
> Trade-name *Cubaris*/*Merulanella* and several tropical taxa are class **b/c** — husbandry-sourced,
> not primary literature.

## Part I — Framing: why multiple schemes?

- No single hierarchy captures a species. *Armadillidium vulgare* is simultaneously: a
  crinochete oniscid (taxonomy), a "roller" (ecomorphology), a fully terrestrial xeric-tolerant
  form (terrestrialization), a grassland/synanthropic detritivore (ecology), a Wolbachia model
  (research use), and a beginner cleanup crew (hobby).
- **Best practice:** pick a small number of *robust, standard* axes as the backbone, then add
  applied/hobby axes on top. Robust = defined in the literature, reproducible, and stable.

## Part II — Categorization schemes (the "many ways")

### 1. Taxonomic / phylogenetic
- **Scheme:** Linnaean hierarchy — Order Isopoda → suborders → families → genera → species.
- **Suborders (11):** Oniscidea (terrestrial), Asellota (mostly freshwater/deep-sea),
  Sphaeromatidea, Cymothoida (incl. fish parasites), Valvifera, Limnoriidea (wood-borers),
  Microcerberidea, Phreatoicidea, Calabozoidea, Tainisopidea, Phoratopidea.
- **Within Oniscidea — evolutionary grades/sections:** Diplocheta (Ligiidae), Tylida,
  Microcheta, Synocheta, Crinocheta (~80% of species diversity; the large derived clade).
  > [!warning] Grades ≠ settled phylogeny (corrected 2026-07-25)
  > These five are **grades/sections**, not a linear ancestor→descendant sequence. Several
  > molecular studies **failed to recover Oniscidea as monophyletic** — Dimitriou, Taiti &
  > Sfenthourakis (2019, *Scientific Reports* 9:18508); Lins et al. (2017) — with *Ligia*/
  > Diplocheta sometimes falling outside a clade containing the other sections. Present the grade
  > sequence as a **traditional morphological framework under active phylogenetic revision**,
  > not confirmed phylogeny.
- **Diversity (cite as a moving target):** fixed 2015 baseline — Sfenthourakis & Taiti (2015,
  *ZooKeys* 515:13–25): **3,710 species / 527 genera / 37 families** (data to Apr 2014).
  Current — OniscidBase / Gardini et al. (2024): **~4,100 species / ~568 genera / 38–39 families**
  (WoRMS updates continuously — always cite with an access date). This vault's tree holds 4,226
  GBIF-accepted names.
- **Authorities:** WoRMS *World List of Marine, Freshwater and Terrestrial Isopod Crustaceans*
  (AphiaID 1131); Schmalfuss *World Catalog of Terrestrial Isopods* (2003); GBIF; Catalogue of Life.
- **Research value:** the reference frame everything else hangs on; needed for synonymy & nomenclature.

### 2. Ecomorphological type (Schmalfuss strategies) ★
- **Scheme:** body-form strategies against predation/desiccation. Full citation: **Schmalfuss, H.
  (1984) "Eco-morphological strategies in terrestrial isopods," in Sutton & Holdich (eds),
  *The Biology of Terrestrial Isopods*, Symp. Zool. Soc. Lond. 53:49–63.** Five strategies:
  **Runner** (fast, flat — *Philoscia*, *Porcellionides pruinosus*), **Clinger** (flattened,
  presses to substrate — *Porcellio scaber/dilatatus*, *Trachelipus*), **Roller/conglobator**
  (spheres — *Armadillidium*, *Cylisticus convexus*), **Creeper** (slow, endogeic, cryptic —
  *Haplophthalmus*, *Androniscus*), **Spiny form** (tropical, armored).
- **Plus a residual bin:** Schmalfuss also defined **"non-conformists"** for species fitting none
  (e.g. *Platyarthrus hoffmannseggii*, *Trichorhina tomentosa*). A **sixth "jumper" type** is a
  *post-1984* addition (Hassall et al. 2006, for a *Burmoniscus*), not part of the original five.
- **Why it's powerful:** a *functional* classification that cuts across taxonomy and predicts
  microhabitat, defense, and behavior; even internal anatomy (lobed tegumental glands) tracks it.
  Conglobation is one visible axis of this.
- **Research value:** convergent-evolution studies; predicting ecology from morphology.

### 3. Degree of terrestrialization ★
- **Scheme:** a gradient of independence from water — from **littoral/supralittoral** (Ligia,
  Tylos) → **hygrophilous** (riparian, cave) → **mesophilous** (forest/leaf litter) →
  **xerophilous** (desert — Hemilepistus).
- **Diagnostic traits:** pleopodal lungs / pseudotracheae development, water-conducting
  systems, cuticle permeability, nitrogen excretion (ammonia gas volatilization).
- **Research value:** a central theme in isopod biology — the crustacean conquest of land.

### 4. Respiratory / physiological adaptation
- **Scheme:** by respiratory apparatus (gill-like pleopods → partial → fully developed
  pseudotracheal "lungs"), water-balance strategy, thermal & desiccation tolerance.
- **Research value:** ties morphology to habitat; basis for climate-response prediction.

### 5. Habitat & microhabitat (stratum)
- **Macrohabitat:** marine · freshwater · terrestrial (forest, grassland, desert, cave,
  coastal/dune, synanthropic/urban).
- **Microhabitat / vertical stratum:** endogeic (soil) · epigeic (surface & litter) ·
  corticolous (under bark) · arboreal · cavernicolous (troglobitic) · commensal (in burrows/nests).
- **Research value:** community ecology, niche partitioning, sampling design.

### 6. Biome / climate zone
- **Scheme:** temperate · Mediterranean · tropical · desert · coastal (plus montane, cave).
- **Research value:** care clustering (hobby), biogeographic and climate-change analysis.

### 7. Biogeography, endemism & invasion
- **Scheme:** native range, endemism (narrow-range cave/island endemics), and
  **cosmopolitan "tramp" species** spread by humans (*P. scaber*, *P. laevis*,
  *A. vulgare*, *Porcellionides pruinosus*).
- **Research value:** island biogeography, invasion ecology, conservation prioritization.

### 8. Trophic / functional guild
- **Scheme:** virtually all Oniscidea are **general detritivores/saprophages** on decaying
  litter, wood and microbial biofilm; most are facultatively coprophagous and take carrion/protein.
  Specialists: fungivores, coprophages (*Platyarthrus* on ant-nest detritus), algivores (littoral
  *Tylos* on beach-cast algae).
  > [!warning] Terrestrial isopods are NOT true xylophages (corrected 2026-07-25)
  > **Xylophagy sensu stricto** (obligate wood-boring) does *not* occur in Oniscidea — they are
  > wood-**associated** detritivores. True xylophagy is marine (*Limnoria*, *Sphaeroma*). Do not
  > code any Oniscidea as an obligate xylophage. Extreme **parasitism** (Cymothoidae on fish,
  > Bopyridae on crustaceans) exists only in the wider order, never in terrestrial isopods.
- **Digestion:** lignocellulose handled largely by gut/hepatopancreatic symbionts — *Candidatus*
  Hepatoplasma crinochetorum (Mollicutes) & *Ca.* Hepatincola porcellionum (Wang, Brune & Zimmer
  2004, 2007). **Copper** (haemocyanin) and **calcium** (cuticle) are physiologically central;
  coprophagy re-inoculates gut microbiota and recovers copper (near-obligate for the microbiome).
- **Ecosystem function:** "soil bioengineers" — litter fragmentation, nutrient cycling, fecal-pellet
  microbial stimulation.
- **Research value:** decomposition/soil ecology; ecosystem-services framing.

### 9. Life-history & reproductive strategy
- **Scheme:** sexual vs **parthenogenetic** (*Trichorhina tomentosa*); brooding in a marsupium
  (all peracarids); **biparental care & subsociality** (*Hemilepistus reaumuri* — desert
  family units); voltinism, longevity, r/K tendencies.
- **Research value:** evolution of sex, parental care, sociality.

### 10. Behavioral
- **Scheme:** aggregation, thigmotaxis, hygrokinesis/photokinesis, sheltering, conglobation
  as defense, turn-alternation behavior (a classic learning/navigation assay).
- **Research value:** behavioral ecology; standard lab assays.

### 11. Symbiotic & host associations
- **Scheme:** **myrmecophily** (ant-nest inquilines — *Platyarthrus*), termitophily,
  vertebrate-burrow commensals; and internally, **Wolbachia** endosymbionts.
- **Research value:** symbiosis, sex-ratio distortion (see Part III-F).

### 12. Conservation status
- **Scheme:** IUCN/national red lists; narrow-range cave & island endemics; legally protected
  species (e.g. *Armadillidium pictum* protected in parts of Europe).
- **Research value:** prioritization; the "dark taxa" problem (undescribed diversity).

### 13. Applied / hobby
- **Scheme:** husbandry care class (temp/humidity/substrate/difficulty), **bioactive role**
  (cleanup crew / micro-cleanup / feeder / display / specialist), trade status
  (described vs undescribed, morphs/cultivars, localities, market value), and
  **bioindication/ecotoxicology** use.
- **Research value:** the bridge between science and the keeping hobby (this vault's niche).

## Part III — Research approaches (the "ways to research")

### A. Taxonomy & systematics
- Dichotomous & interactive **identification keys**; comparison with **type specimens**;
  **integrative taxonomy** (morphology + molecules); revisionary monographs.
- Character systems: cephalic lobes, antennae, pereopods, pleopod/lung structure, uropods,
  male pleopod 1–2 (genitalia), cuticular microstructure (SEM).

### B. Field ecology & sampling
- **Pitfall traps** (epigeic activity), **litter sifting** + **Berlese/Tullgren funnels**
  (extraction), **hand collection / quadrats / transects**, bark & log searches, cave surveys,
  soil cores (endogeic), **mark–recapture** for populations.
- Standardized surveys for community composition, abundance, phenology.

### C. Laboratory culture & experiments
- Rearing/breeding lines; controlled temp/humidity/substrate trials; growth & molt tracking;
  common-garden and reciprocal-transplant designs.

### D. Physiology & ecophysiology
- **Desiccation-resistance** assays, **thermal-tolerance** (CTmax/CTmin), **respirometry**,
  water-balance & cuticle-permeability measurement, ammonia-volatilization studies.

### E. Behavior
- Choice chambers (humidity/light/shelter gradients), aggregation & shelter-use assays,
  **turn-alternation** mazes, activity rhythms.

### F. Molecular & genomic ★
- **DNA barcoding** (mitochondrial COI) for ID & cryptic-species discovery; multi-gene &
  phylogenomic trees; population genetics/phylogeography.
- **Wolbachia** research: feminization & sex-ratio distortion in *Armadillidium vulgare* — a
  flagship system (Poitiers group: Juchault, Rigaud, Bouchon, Cordaux). Includes the famous
  **feminizing "f element"** integrated into the host genome.
- Epigenetics & sex-determination; genome/transcriptome studies of terrestrialization.

### G. Ecotoxicology & bioindication ★
- ***Porcellio scaber*** is the **de-facto standard** terrestrial-isopod test organism:
  **heavy-metal bioaccumulation** in the hepatopancreas (Zn/Cd/Pb/Cu in B-cell granules — Hopkin
  1989/1990; Dallinger & Prosi 1988), feeding-rate assays (Drobne & Hopkin 1995), avoidance tests
  (adapted from earthworm ISO 17512-1), and "woodlouse watch" field biomonitoring.
  > [!warning] No ratified isopod test guideline (corrected 2026-07-25)
  > There is **NO ratified ISO or OECD test guideline for terrestrial isopods** — ratified
  > guidelines exist only for earthworms (OECD 207/222; ISO 17512-1) and springtails (OECD 232;
  > ISO 17512-2). Isopods are the leading *candidate* for a future standard (van Gestel, Loureiro &
  > Zidar 2018, *ZooKeys* 801:127–162). Call *P. scaber* a "de-facto model," not an "ISO/OECD test
  > organism."

### H. Data science, databases & citizen science
- **WoRMS** (authoritative names/synonymy), **GBIF** (occurrences/ranges), **iNaturalist**
  (citizen-science observations & images), museum collections, literature (ZooKeys and other
  taxonomic journals).
- Trait databases; niche/species-distribution modeling (SDMs); occurrence cleaning & analysis.

## Part IV — Recommended backbone axes (the "best" ways)

For a durable, analyzable vault, adopt these standard/robust axes first, then layer hobby data:

1. **Taxonomy** (already built) — the reference frame.
2. **Ecomorphological type** (Schmalfuss) — the single most useful *functional* axis; subsumes
   conglobation.
3. **Degree of terrestrialization / moisture niche** — central to isopod biology.
4. **Habitat & microhabitat stratum** — drives ecology and sampling.
5. **Biogeography** (native range + tramp/endemic status).
6. **Trophic/functional guild** — for the decomposer-ecology framing.
7. **Life-history/reproductive mode** — flags the standout biology (parthenogenesis, sociality).

Then the **applied layer**: husbandry class, bioactive role, trade/morph status. These are the
axes I sketched for the "Isopod Atlas" facet system — the outline above shows which are
*scientifically standard* (1–7) vs *hobby-specific* (applied).

- **iNaturalist** — citizen-science observations, images, range hints.
- **Schmalfuss, H.** — *World Catalog of Terrestrial Isopods* (2003); "Eco-morphological

  *PNAS* 113:15036–15041, "Birth of a W sex chromosome…")**; Cordaux & Gilbert (2017, *Genes* 8:186).

  Drobne & Hopkin (1995).

  Hornung (2011, *Terrestrial Arthropod Reviews* 4:95–130).

- **Databases:** WoRMS (AphiaID 1131), GBIF, iNaturalist, OniscidBase.
- Hobby/community: AIMG checklists; isopod.site (trade-name convention); keeper forums/wikis for
  trade names & morphs (treat as unverified for taxonomy).

> [!warning] Citation caveats (from the verification pass)
> - **Nomenclatural trap** (both in this vault): *Porcellio hoffmannseggi* (Porcellionidae, Iberian
>   giant) ≠ *Platyarthrus hoffmannseggii* (Platyarthridae, blind ant-associate) — both abbreviate
>   "*P. hoffmannseggii*." Never conflate.
> - A few paginations were reconstructed from secondary citations (e.g. Drobne & Hopkin 1995,
>   *Ecotox. Environ. Saf.* 31:89–99) — confirm before formal use.
> - Species counts are a moving target; WoRMS was bot-blocked to direct fetch, so counts came from
>   WoRMS-derived mirrors + recent papers. Cite with an access date.

> [!warning] Verify before citing
> Author/year attributions above are pointers from general knowledge. Confirm exact citations
> and current taxonomic opinions in the primary literature or WoRMS before using them formally.

## Part VI — Frontiers & open questions

- **Dark taxa:** vast undescribed diversity (tropical *Cubaris*/*Merulanella* "sp.", cave
  endemics) — the hobby is trading animals science hasn't named.
- **Wolbachia & sex determination:** genome integration, feminization mechanisms.
- **Terrestrialization genomics:** how crustaceans solved life on land (respiration, water, N).
- **Climate & urban ecology:** range shifts, tramp-species spread, city tolerance.
- **Bioindication:** isopods as sentinels for soil metals/microplastics.
- **Functional ecology:** quantifying their role in decomposition & soil carbon.
